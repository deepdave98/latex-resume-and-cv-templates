# Graduate admissions CV

For PhD and research-based master's applications, with prompts suited to computing and quantitative research. [Source](../templates/graduate-admissions-resume.tex) · [Starter ZIP](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepdave98/latex-resume-and-cv-templates/main/downloads/graduate-admissions-resume.zip) · [PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepdave98/latex-resume-and-cv-templates/main/output/pdf/graduate-admissions-resume.pdf).

## Check the program first

Check whether a CV is requested, its page limit, and required fields. Oxford removes unrequested CVs and asks for one or two pages where required. [Oxford application guide](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/graduate/application-guide/supporting-documents/cv-resume).

This starter fits one page. Add a second only if your content and the program's rules warrant it; change `\resumepagelimit{1}` to `{2}`. Do not shrink the type to force a fit.

Put your research direction and reasons for applying in the statement of purpose. [CMU SCS requirements](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/education/graduate-admissions).

## What to keep

- **Education first:** degree, institution, completion or expected date. Include a thesis title and supervisor if applicable. A prior master's is not assumed.
- **Research next:** question, your contribution, method, and finding or limitation. Name the supervisor and dates. A lab's project description does not explain your part.
- **Projects:** label course projects and independent study accurately. Keep those that show preparation for the proposed field; separate your work from teammates' work.
- **Teaching, service, awards, skills:** retain relevant entries you actually have. For awards, name the awarding body and year. Delete empty sections.

This order follows the education/research emphasis in [Stanford PhD students' applicant guide](https://www.cs.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj28076/files/media/file/general-advice-for-cs-phd-applicants.pdf), not a university-mandated layout. Within sections, put the most recent work first.

For a taught or professional master's, adapt the emphasis to its selection criteria: relevant coursework, projects, and employment may replace research entries. Do not turn a class assignment into an RA appointment.

## Research without a paper

Keep substantive thesis or research work even if it has not produced a publication. MIT Physics explicitly says publications are not necessary for admission to its program. [Research and publication guidance](https://physics.mit.edu/academic-programs/graduate-students/graduate-admissions-additional-guidance/).

For unfinished work, state the completed method or analysis and what remains unresolved. Include negative findings with the conditions you tested.

Illustrative rewrite, not a claim to copy:

> Implemented the paper's baseline and repeated the comparison across five random seeds. The reported improvement did not persist across runs; documented the variance and differences from the published setup.

Use this only if it describes your work.

## Papers and presentations

The source includes commented-out blocks. Uncomment only what applies.

- Separate published/accepted work from preprints and submissions. A draft sent to your supervisor is not a journal submission. “Under review” is not “accepted.”
- Preserve author order. Give the title, year, venue where applicable, and current status. Follow the program's citation instructions; link only work you can share.
- Label a poster or talk with its event and date. Attending an event is not presenting at it. Do not count a poster as a peer-reviewed paper.

MIT Physics asks applicants to distinguish publication status and presentation type. Its instructions concern its own application fields, not every program's CV. [Details](https://physics.mit.edu/academic-programs/graduate-students/graduate-admissions-additional-guidance/).

Before submitting, check dates and titles against your transcript, statement, and application form. Replace every bracketed prompt, verify links, and read the exported PDF. Do not include confidential data or unpublished coauthors' work without permission.
